Abstract

Homework assistance involves decisions about both what a child needs and who can determine how help is provided. We examine these decisions through an exploratory, AI-assisted secondary analysis of 16 episodes from 15 Mandarin homework sessions involving 12 family identifiers, selected from an existing collection of 602 session files. Using epistemic and deontic authority as an analytical lens, we distinguish claims to knowledge from rights to determine action. The episodes illustrate three tensions: knowing the material does not settle children's accounts of their learning needs; offering a choice may not address the decision a child contests; and task progress can coexist with disagreement about assistance. Contrasting cases show that taking up children's contributions can involve continued guidance rather than withdrawal. We develop the contestability of help as a design orientation for family artificial intelligence (AI), emphasizing opportunities to question assistance's grounds, scope, and continuation and receive a relevant response. We propose matching choices to disputed decisions, separating content correction from intervention decisions, and making responses to objections observable. These are design implications from selected human interactions, not validated effects of an AI intervention. The study contributes a situated account of how assistance can remain open to revision without treating either expertise or task completion as sufficient to settle its terms.

Selected interaction cases

Three paper-grounded examples with Mandarin excerpts, English translations, and explicit interpretive limits. Release identifiers are unrelated to the original family identifiers. These excerpts are not full conversational sequences.

CASE A

Choosing a recipient—or refusing to share?

Decision scope

A parent proposed showing a recording of homework behavior to a teacher. The child challenged sharing itself.

Child

“I don't want that.”

我不要。

Parent

“You decide whom I should send it to.”

你决定让我发给谁。

Child

“You can't send it to anyone.”

谁都不能发。

Analytical reading. The offered choice and the child's objection concerned different levels of decision.

Limit. The record does not establish whether anything was recorded or sent in response.

CASE B

Following the child's method

Local uptake

During a money problem, the parent invited the child's route through the calculation and asked questions within it.

Parent

“Wait, then I'll follow your method. Explain it to me.”

等会啊那我按照你的方法来，你给我讲讲。

[Intervening explanation and questions omitted]

Parent

“I understand it now.”

我听懂了。

Analytical reading. Taking up the child's contribution did not require the parent to stop guiding.

Limit. This does not establish that the parent previously lacked the answer or that learning improved. These selected quotes include only the parent; the intervening child explanation is not released.

CASE C

The passage is finished. The disagreement is not.

Progress ≠ agreement

After assisted reading, the parent recognized completion. The child continued to describe a preference for independent reading.

Parent

“Okay, you read it through. Good.”

行，那读下来了，不错。

Child

“See? I can read it myself.”

对吧？我自己能读也能读下来。

[Further discussion about the recording omitted]

Child

“I want to rely on myself, rather than on that recording.”

因为我想靠我自己，而不是靠那个录音。

Analytical reading. Recognized task progress and acceptance of the help arrangement were distinct.

Limit. The recording is not an AI system. Neither the parent's assessment nor the child's account is an independent learning measure.

Designing for contestable help

Design implications to develop and evaluate with families—not tested interface features.

01

Match the decision

Distinguish whether to help now from which hint to provide. Respond at the level of the child's objection.

02

Separate knowing from deciding

A correct answer does not settle the method, timing, or scope of assistance. Make these grounds discussable.

03

Make the response visible

Show what changed after an objection and explain any retained requirement. Continued use alone is not agreement.
